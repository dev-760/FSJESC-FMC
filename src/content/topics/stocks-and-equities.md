# Stocks and what a share actually entitles you to

*For the members of the Financial Market Club, FSJES*

When an investor buys a share on the Bourse de Casablanca, what do they actually own? They do not own a proportional slice of company furniture, server rooms, or physical offices. They own a junior, residual claim on future cash distributions, positioned strictly behind every creditor, supplier, and tax authority.

## The distribution hierarchy: why equity is residual

A company issues two primary types of securities: debt and equity. Debt is a legal contract requiring fixed, timely payments. Equity is a residual claim.

If a Moroccan joint-stock company (*societe anonyme*) is wound up or liquidates its assets, the law establishes a strict hierarchy:
1. Secured bank lenders and employee wage claims.
2. Unsecured bondholders and trade creditors.
3. Preferred shareholders (*actions a dividende prioritaire sans droit de vote*).
4. Ordinary shareholders (*actions ordinaires*).

Ordinary shareholders receive only what remains after every prior claim is fully satisfied. That is why equity carries the highest risk among standard market instruments: in a severe business failure, common shareholders routinely receive zero. In exchange for this subordinate status, ordinary shareholders retain full voting rights at annual general meetings (*Assemblees Generales*) and unlimited upside participation if company profits grow.

## Why do Moroccan companies pay dividends?

A firm distributes cash to shareholders for one reason: it has generated more free cash flow than management can reinvest at attractive rates of return.

On the Bourse de Casablanca, dividend yield is a primary investment criterion. Large capitalization stalwarts like Maroc Telecom (IAM), Attijariwafa Bank, and Banque Centrale Populaire (BCP) have historically maintained high payout ratios, returning 70% to 100% of annual net income to shareholders. For an investor, a dividend is a realization of cash earnings. For a mature firm, paying a substantial dividend signals reliable cash generation, while a high-growth SME on the Casablanca exchange's alternative compartment may retain all earnings to finance network expansion.

A common beginner mistake is treating the dividend yield as the entire investment return. Total return equals the dividend yield plus the capital gain or loss from share price movement.

## Why share prices move more than company fundamentals

Equity prices discount cash flows extending decades into the future. A small change in the discount rate applied to those distant cash flows produces a large swing in the share price, even if company revenue is completely unchanged.

Consider a mature company expected to produce a steady cash flow of 10 dirhams per share indefinitely. If investors discount those cash flows at an 8% expected rate of return, the theoretical value is:

$$\text{Price} = \frac{\text{Cash Flow}}{\text{Discount Rate}} = \frac{10}{0.08} = 125 \text{ MAD}$$

If macroeconomic uncertainty or rising Treasury bond yields push the required rate of return to 9%, the share price adjusts:

$$\text{Price} = \frac{10}{0.09} = 111.11 \text{ MAD}$$

The business itself did not change, its customers paid their bills, and factory output was constant. Yet the share price fell by over 11% purely due to a 100-basis-point adjustment in market discount rates.

## Capital increases and dilution

When a listed company seeks new funds for development without taking bank loans, it executes a capital increase (*augmentation de capital*) by issuing new shares. This introduces two distinct operations:

- **Primary issuance:** The company creates new shares. Existing shareholders must either exercise their preferential subscription rights (*droits preferentiels de souscription*) or face dilution of their fractional ownership. The company receives fresh cash on its balance sheet.
- **Secondary transactions:** An existing founding family or private equity fund sells existing shares to the public on the Bourse. The company itself receives zero dirhams; only the ownership of existing claims changes hands.

The Autorite Marocaine du Marche des Capitaux (AMMC) regulates these transactions through mandatory prospectuses (*notes d'information*), ensuring retail and institutional investors receive audited balance sheets and risk disclosures before subscription.
